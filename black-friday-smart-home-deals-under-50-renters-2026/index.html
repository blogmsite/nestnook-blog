What NOT to buy this Black Friday

1. The Echo Dot — at $79.99.

The default renter speaker jumped from $49.99 to $79.99 this year (TrustJaguar, early November 2026): "Memory chip shortages pushed some 2026 prices up, so the Echo Dot ($79.99 list) no longer makes the cut" for under-$50 lists, and "the biggest casualty for gift shoppers is the Echo Dot, which jumped from $49.99 to $79.99." The new 2026 Echo Dot is a good device — built-in Zigbee/Matter/Thread hub, Alexa+, eero extender — but at $79.99 it is not a budget buy. If you want a speaker, the Google Home Speaker is $79.99 too (sale 2 Nov–2 Dec), and the Echo Show 5 was $49.99 on Prime Day — watch for it to return under $50 during Black Friday Week.

2. The Tapo and Kasa Matter dimmers — if you rent.

The Tapo Matter Smart Dimmer is $12.99 (35% off) and the Kasa Matter Smart Dimmer is $16.99 (32% off) — both genuine deals (SammyGuru, 11 November 2026). But both replace a wall switch and require a neutral wire. That is an electrician's job in a flat you do not own. Buy the Linkind bulbs instead: same Matter ecosystem, no wiring.

3. Any "68% off" robot vacuum without checking the price history.

Koupon lists the Lefant M210 OMNI at $159.99 with a code — "68% off" a $499.99 list price (12 November 2026). Gavler's price-history brief (12 November 2026) warns about exactly this pattern: the SwitchBot K11+ sells at $399.99 today but "sold for $200 with a SwitchBot coupon in July (Slickdeals), so $400 is not the real price". A discount off an inflated list price is not a deal. Check the price history (CamelCamelCamel, Slickdeals, or the brand's own sale page) before you believe a percentage.

4. Anything from a sale that ends today — unless you actually want it.

SwitchBot's up-to-70%-off robot vacuum codes end today, 14 November. Urgency is real here, but it is also the oldest sales trick there is. If you were already going to buy a SwitchBot, buy it today. If you were not, the deadline is not a reason to start.

The subscription trap: the device price is only part of the cost

This is the line most deal roundups skip, and it matters more for renters than for anyone else — because renters move, and subscriptions do not move with you cleanly.

  • Ring and Blink need a paid plan to save video history. Ring recording starts at $4.99/month for one camera. A $39.99 doorbell plus a year of recording is $39.99 + ~$60 — the subscription costs more than the device in year one.
  • Wyze, Reolink and eufy record locally. Wyze Cam v4, Reolink cameras and eufy SoloCam record to a card or base station with no mandatory subscription. That is the renter hook: buy the hardware once, own the footage.
  • Apple's version is cheaper than Google's and Amazon's. HomeKit Secure Video starts with iCloud+ at $0.99/month for one camera; Google Home Premium is $10/month (Standard) or $20/month (Advanced); Alexa+ is $20/month or included with Prime (Skimwire, 13 November 2026).
  • Check before you buy: does the camera record anything without a plan? Can you view live video free? Can you download clips? If the answer to all three is no, the "$39.99 deal" is a $100 first-year commitment.

The renter rules (the 60-second version)

  1. No drilling, no wiring, no electrician. If it plugs in, sticks on, screws into an existing fitting or replaces a peephole, it is renter-safe. If it needs a neutral wire, a junction box or a screwdriver aimed at anything structural, skip it.
  2. Everything must leave with you. Smart plugs, bulbs, battery cameras, a battery doorbell and a plug-in speaker pack into a moving box. A wired floodlight and a replaced wall switch do not.
  3. Check the price history, not the percentage. A "68% off" tag off an inflated list price is marketing. A lowest-ever price from a tracked source is a deal.
  4. Count the subscription before you count the saving. A $39.99 camera with a $4.99/month plan costs $99.87 in year one. A $36 camera with local storage costs $36.
  5. Keep the originals. Old bulbs, the old peephole, the landlord's lock hardware — bag them, label them, reinstall them at move-out.
