How to stop package theft in an apartment (holiday 2026)

A video doorbell is not just about knowing who knocked. For apartment renters, its real job is package theft — and the numbers explain why November is the moment to buy one.

  • Security.org’s 2026 Package Theft Report (survey of 3,494 US adults, updated 28 October 2026) estimates 47.7 million Americans had a package stolen in the last year, worth $7.7 billion — and apartment residents were more than three times as likely to have a package stolen in the past three months (10% vs 3% of single-family homes). 45% of apartment residents have ever had a package stolen, against 25% of people in houses.
  • Parcel Pending’s holiday guide (updated 4 November 2026) reports apartment residents are nearly three times as likely to experience package theft, and holiday deliveries to apartment communities can rise by as much as 81% a week in the peak period. Capital One Shopping research puts more than 30% of Americans as package-theft victims in the past year, with 39% of stolen packages worth over $100.
  • Omnisend’s 2026 figures put roughly 48% of annual package thefts in November and December — the holiday delivery peak.

What a doorbell actually does about it: it records the delivery, it lets you talk to the courier through two-way talk (“please leave it with the concierge”), and it gives you a clip with a time stamp if a parcel disappears. The head-to-toe view on the 2K models matters here — a standard camera films faces; a head-to-toe camera also films the parcel on the floor. If your building has a parcel room or a concierge, add a note in the delivery instructions and use the doorbell to confirm the handover happened.

How to stop package theft in an apartment: the 5-step renter plan

  1. Fit the doorbell first — it records every delivery and lets you talk to the courier live through two-way talk.
  2. Write delivery instructions on every order: “Leave with concierge / parcel room / neighbour at [flat], do not leave outside the door.”
  3. Use your building’s parcel locker if it has one — Parcel Pending’s locker research shows lockers virtually eliminate theft, and residents rank them the top amenity. If your building has none, ask management — it is the single most effective fix.
  4. Ship to a pick-up point for anything valuable — Amazon Lockers, store pick-up, or your workplace.
  5. Ask a neighbour to collect parcels on delivery day, and return the favour. DoorVi’s renter guide (30 October 2026) has more delivery-day tactics.

Renter tip: aim the camera at the floor, not the sky

Mount the doorbell at chest height and tilt it slightly down. In a shared hallway, that keeps the frame on visitors and parcels — and keeps your neighbours’ doors mostly out of shot, which is both politer and better for your landlord conversation.

Best smart lock for renters: pair your doorbell with a keyless lock

A doorbell watches the door; a smart lock controls it. Together they are the renter security pair: the doorbell tells you a parcel arrived, the lock lets you in without fumbling for keys — and both come off cleanly when you move. The rule is the same as for doorbells: retrofit only. A retrofit lock clamps over the thumb-turn on the inside of your existing deadbolt, so the outside of the door, the keyhole and your landlord’s key never change. Anything that replaces the deadbolt itself needs written permission first.

How to ask your landlord (copy-paste script)

Most leases do not ban a battery doorbell outright — they ban drilling, permanent changes and anything that damages the door. A no-drill doorbell avoids all three, but asking first is still the smart move: it creates a paper trail, and a landlord who has said yes in writing cannot deduct for it later. Send it by email or text so you have the reply saved.

Copy-paste landlord message

“Hi [landlord’s name], I’d like to fit a small battery video doorbell at the front door of [flat number]. It mounts with adhesive — no drilling, no screws, no wiring — and I’ll remove it and make good when I move out. It only films the hallway directly outside my door. Would you be happy for me to go ahead? Happy to send a photo of the mount first if helpful. Thanks, [your name].”

If your door has a peephole, use this version instead: “I’d like to swap the existing peephole for a Ring Peephole Cam, which fits into the same hole with no drilling. I’ll keep the original peephole safe and refit it before I move out.”

If they say no, ask what they would accept — a peephole camera is often the compromise, because nothing is drilled, stuck or wired.

Two rules that protect your deposit: never film inside a neighbour’s flat or a shared bathroom, and check your building’s rules — some blocks ban cameras in shared hallways entirely, in which case a peephole camera is usually still fine because it films only your own doorway.

Moving out: remove it in 10 minutes, keep your deposit

The whole point of a renter doorbell is that it leaves with you. When moving day comes:

  1. Adhesive mounts: warm the pad with a hairdryer for 30 seconds, then peel slowly. Wipe the residue with a damp cloth — no marks, no holes.
  2. Peephole camera: unscrew the indoor unit, slide the camera out, and refit the original peephole you kept in a drawer. Five minutes.
  3. Factory-reset the doorbell in the app before you hand it to the next tenant or sell it, and remove it from your Ring or Blink account.
  4. Photograph the door after removal, so you have proof it is undamaged if the deposit is ever questioned.
  5. Take it with you. A battery doorbell is one of the few smart home devices that moves flat to flat with zero re-installation cost — which is exactly why buying a good one now is money well spent.

Renter doorbell FAQ

Can I install a video doorbell in a rented apartment?

Yes, in most cases — as long as you do not drill, wire or permanently change the door. Battery doorbells on adhesive mounts and peephole cameras are the renter-safe options. Ask your landlord first in writing, using the script above, and keep their reply.

What is the best video doorbell for an apartment?

For most renters, the Ring Battery Doorbell 2K Plus — the sharpest video, night vision, quick-release battery, and the biggest Black Friday 2026 discount ($79.99–$99.99 verified 8–9 November 2026). If your flat has a peephole and no drilling is allowed at all, the Ring Peephole Cam 2K is the better fit. On a tight budget, the Ring Battery Doorbell 2K at $39.99–$44.99 or the Blink Battery Doorbell 2K+ at $35.99 are the best value picks.

Do I need a subscription for a video doorbell?

Not for live view, motion alerts or two-way talk — those are free. You need a plan only to save and rewatch clips: roughly $4–$5 a month for a single Ring device, or $3.99 a month for Blink Basic. Blink is the exception: with a Sync Module and a USB stick, clips store locally and no subscription is needed.

Will a video doorbell work with weak hallway Wi-Fi?

Usually, yes — but test first. Stand at your front door with your phone and check the Wi-Fi signal. If it is weak, move your router closer to the door, or add a cheap Wi-Fi extender inside the flat. Blink models only use 2.4 GHz Wi-Fi, so make sure that band is switched on in your router settings.

Can my landlord stop me fitting one?

They can refuse drilling or permanent changes, and some buildings ban hallway cameras. A battery or peephole model that causes no damage is much harder to refuse, and most landlords agree when asked in writing. If yours refuses, ask what they would accept — the peephole camera is the usual compromise.

Is the Ring Door View Cam still worth buying?

No — Ring discontinued the original 2019 Door View Cam, and you cannot buy it new. Its replacement is the Ring Peephole Cam 2K (August 2026, $99.99), with sharper 2K video and a lower price. Any guide still recommending the Door View Cam is out of date.

What is the best smart lock for renters?

A retrofit lock that clamps over your existing deadbolt without drilling: the Aqara Smart Lock U200 ($169.99 list) for Apple Home households, or the SwitchBot Lock Ultra ($139.99) for the lowest price. The Schlage Sense Pro ($399) replaces the whole deadbolt, so it needs written landlord permission.

How do I stop package theft in an apartment?

Fit a video doorbell with a head-to-toe view, write delivery instructions on every order, use your building’s parcel locker or a pick-up point for valuable items, and ask a neighbour to collect parcels on delivery day. Apartment residents are more than three times as likely to have a package stolen as people in houses (Security.org, 2026).
