The short answer

A retrofit smart lock does not replace your deadbolt. It clamps onto the thumb-turn on the inside of your existing deadbolt and turns it for you — by phone, by code, by fingerprint or automatically as you arrive. The outside of the door looks identical, the cylinder is untouched, and your landlord's key still works exactly as before. When you move out, it unclamps in about ten minutes and the original thumb-turn goes back on.

That is the whole renter test in one sentence, and it is also the reason a retrofit lock is the one smart-home upgrade most landlords say yes to. The three picks below cover every budget and every phone:

  • Best overall: August Wi-Fi Smart Lock (4th Gen) — list $229.99, Yale's own price $199.99, often around $110–$120 on Amazon. Wi-Fi built in, no hub needed, works with Alexa, Google Home, Apple Home and SmartThings.
  • Best budget: SwitchBot Lock Pro — around $100, and the only pick here that mounts with 3M adhesive: no tools, no screws, and it peels off cleanly at move-out.
  • Best for iPhone homes: Aqara U200 — list $229.99, often around $189 on Amazon. Fingerprint reader on the interior unit, Apple Home Key, and Matter over Thread. No monthly subscription on any of the five picks for basic locking.

Bottom line for renters: if you want one lock and you want it to just work, buy the August 4th Gen. If your budget is tight or your lease bans even screwdriver work, buy the SwitchBot Lock Pro — adhesive mount, about $100, and it leaves no trace. If your home runs on Apple, buy the Aqara U200 and unlock with your iPhone or Apple Watch.

What makes a lock renter-safe

Most smart locks are not renter-safe. The popular ones — Yale Assure Lock 2, Schlage Encode Plus, Desloc B200 — replace the whole deadbolt, including the cylinder your landlord's key fits. That is a change to the landlord's property, and it needs explicit permission. A retrofit lock passes four tests that a replacement lock fails:

  1. No drilling. Nothing new is screwed, cut or bored into the door or the frame. Retrofit locks use the deadbolt's existing mounting screws — or, in the SwitchBot Lock Pro's case, 3M adhesive.
  2. The exterior is unchanged. The outside of the door looks exactly as it did before. No new keypad, no new cylinder, no visible smart device for a neighbour or a landlord to object to.
  3. The landlord's key still works. The cylinder is never touched, so the original key opens the door exactly as it always did — including in an emergency, when the battery is dead, or when you have already moved out.
  4. It removes cleanly in about ten minutes. Unclamp it, refit the original thumb-turn, and the door is back to the way you found it. Keep the original thumb-turn in a labelled bag so it is there at move-out.

August Wi-Fi Smart Lock (4th Gen) — best overall

Best overall

August Wi-Fi Smart Lock (4th Gen)

Price: list $229.99; Yale's own price $199.99 (October 2026); often around $110–$120 on Amazon. Hub: none — Wi-Fi is built in. Works with: Alexa, Google Home, Apple Home, SmartThings. Install: about 10 minutes with a screwdriver, using the deadbolt's existing screws.

The August is the retrofit lock that made the category, and the 4th Gen is still the one to beat for most renters. It is the smallest August yet, it connects straight to your Wi-Fi with no hub to buy or plug in, and it talks to every major smart-home platform — which matters in a rental, because you may not know which voice assistant your next flat will use. Auto-unlock as you walk up, auto-lock behind you, guest keys you can hand out and take back, and a full activity log of who came and went.

Pros

  • Wi-Fi built in — no hub, no extra box, remote access out of the box.
  • Works with Alexa, Google Home, Apple Home and SmartThings.
  • Often half its list price on Amazon (~$110–$120).
  • Small, quiet, and installs in about 10 minutes.

Cons

  • Auto-unlock can be inconsistent — some reviewers report it triggering late or not at all.
  • No keypad on the lock body; the keypad is a separate purchase.
  • Battery life (3–6 months on 2× CR123) is shorter than the Yale Approach.

Renter verdict: the safest all-round buy. If you want one lock that works with whatever you already own — and whatever you buy next — this is it.

Yale Approach Lock with Wi-Fi — best auto-lock

Best auto-lock

Yale Approach Lock with Wi-Fi

Price: $139.99 list (Yale's own price, October 2026). Keypad version $179.99; Keypad Touch with fingerprint $239.99. Hub: none — Wi-Fi is built in. Battery: 9–12 months on AA batteries. Works with: Alexa and Google voice assistants.

The Approach is Yale's retrofit answer to the August, and it wins on the two things renters notice every day: it locks itself reliably behind you (Auto-Lock with DoorSense knows whether the door is actually shut), and its AA batteries last 9–12 months — roughly twice the August. It mounts inside over most single-cylinder deadbolts, gives you unlimited virtual keys for guests and cleaners, and the optional keypad means nobody needs a phone to get in. One honest limit: Yale lists Matter as arriving later by software update, and the Approach does not support Apple HomeKit — if your home runs on Apple, the Aqara U200 below is the better pick.

Pros

  • Auto-Lock with DoorSense — it will not lock an open door.
  • 9–12 months of battery life on ordinary AAs.
  • Unlimited virtual keys; keypad and fingerprint keypad options.
  • Wi-Fi built in, no hub.

Cons

  • No Apple HomeKit support — Apple households should look at the Aqara U200.
  • Matter support is promised later, not here yet.
  • Keypad costs extra ($179.99 with keypad, $239.99 with fingerprint keypad).

Renter verdict: the best lock for forgetful humans. If you have ever left the flat wondering whether you locked the door, the Approach answers that question itself.

SwitchBot Lock Pro — best budget

Best budget

SwitchBot Lock Pro

Price: around $100. Hub: only for Matter — basic Bluetooth control works without one. Battery: 6–9 months. Mounting: 3M adhesive — no tools, no screws.

The Lock Pro is the cheapest way into a retrofit lock, and it is also the least invasive: it mounts with 3M adhesive, so there is no screwdriver at all — stick it on, calibrate it in the app, and peel it off cleanly when you move. That makes it the best pick for the strictest leases, the ones that ban even touching the existing hardware. A keypad is available on some bundles, and Matter support comes through a SwitchBot hub if you want it. The trade-off is the ecosystem: it is a Bluetooth-first lock, so remote access and automations are stronger with a hub than without.

Pros

  • Adhesive mount — no tools, no screws, peels off cleanly.
  • Cheapest pick here at around $100.
  • Keypad option on some bundles; Matter via a SwitchBot hub.

Cons

  • Bluetooth-first: remote access is limited without a hub.
  • Adhesive mount needs a clean, flat surface on the door interior.
  • Smaller ecosystem than August or Yale.

Renter verdict: the best lock for a strict lease. If your landlord's rule is "don't touch anything", this is the lock that obeys it — and it costs about $100.

SwitchBot Lock Ultra — best new retrofit

Best new retrofit

SwitchBot Lock Ultra

Price: $139.99 for the lock alone; around $250 with the Keypad Vision. Hub: only for Matter — a SwitchBot hub is needed for Matter support. Battery: around 9 months on a 3×18350 rechargeable pack, plus an emergency cell.

The Lock Ultra is SwitchBot's newer, quieter, more compact retrofit — the Lock Pro's successor in all but price. It turns the thumb-turn faster and quieter, it is smaller on the door, and the optional Keypad Vision adds face unlock alongside fingerprint, code and card. The battery is a rechargeable 3×18350 pack (about 9 months) with an emergency cell as a backup, so a flat battery does not mean a locked-out renter. At $139.99 it sits between the Lock Pro and the Yale Approach; the ~$250 Keypad Vision bundle is the one to buy only if face unlock matters to you.

Pros

  • Quieter and more compact than the Lock Pro.
  • Keypad Vision adds face unlock, fingerprint, code and card.
  • Rechargeable battery pack plus an emergency cell.

Cons

  • Costs ~$40 more than the Lock Pro for the lock alone.
  • Matter still needs a SwitchBot hub.
  • The full face-unlock bundle is around $250.

Renter verdict: the Lock Pro if you are counting dollars; the Lock Ultra if you want the quieter motor and the face-unlock keypad — and you are willing to pay for it.

Aqara U200 — best for Apple Home

Best for Apple Home

Aqara U200

Price: list $229.99; often around $189 on Amazon (launched at $269.99). Hub: a Thread border router (Apple TV 4K or HomePod mini) for remote access. Battery: 6–8 months. Keypad: separate outdoor keypad, mounts with screws or adhesive.

The U200 is the retrofit lock for an Apple household. It is the only pick here with a fingerprint reader on the interior unit, it supports Apple Home Key — unlock with your iPhone or Apple Watch held near the door — and it runs Matter over Thread, so it stays fast and local instead of routing everything through a cloud. The outdoor keypad is a separate unit that mounts with screws or adhesive, and there is no monthly subscription for any of it. The catch is the ecosystem: to use it remotely you need a Thread border router in the flat (an Apple TV 4K or a HomePod mini), and Android households get less out of it than Apple ones.

Pros

  • Apple Home Key — unlock with iPhone or Apple Watch.
  • Fingerprint reader on the interior unit.
  • Matter over Thread: fast, local, no cloud round-trip.
  • No monthly subscription.

Cons

  • Needs a Thread border router for remote access.
  • Outdoor keypad is a separate purchase.
  • Most of the value is in the Apple ecosystem.

Renter verdict: if your home runs on Apple, this is the lock. If it does not, the August or the Yale Approach will serve you better for less money.

Honourable mention: Level Bolt

The Level Bolt is the only retrofit lock that hides inside the deadbolt itself — invisible from both sides of the door, with no visible smart device at all. It is the most discreet option on the market, and the most landlord-friendly to look at, because there is nothing to look at. We have not verified a current price for it — check the current price before you buy. If invisible hardware matters more to you than a keypad or a fingerprint reader, it is worth a look; if you want the most features per dollar, stick with the five picks above.

Locks that are NOT renter-safe without permission

These are good locks — they are simply not retrofit locks. Each one replaces your deadbolt, including the cylinder, so your landlord's key stops working and the outside of the door changes. That is a change to the landlord's property, and it needs explicit permission first — a quick message is not enough, and "I put the old one back" does not undo the change while you live there.

  • Yale Assure Lock 2 — Yale's best-known replacement lock. Great hardware, but it is a full deadbolt swap.
  • Schlage Encode Plus — $399, and the best Home Key lock you can buy outright. Also a full replacement, and the most expensive lock in this guide by a wide margin.
  • Desloc B200 — a strong budget replacement lock, and its Black Friday sale runs to 30 November 2026. Still a replacement: ask first, buy second.

If your heart is set on one of these, use the message template in the next section and get a yes in writing before you order. If the answer is no — or no answer comes — buy a retrofit lock instead. You get the same keyless entry, and the door stays the landlord's.

Ask your landlord first — copy-paste message

Retrofit locks are the easiest smart-home upgrade to get approved, because the landlord loses nothing: their key works, the door is unchanged, and the lock leaves with you. Asking first still matters — it turns a grey area into a yes in writing, and a yes in writing protects your deposit. Send this as a message or an email:

Copy-paste message to your landlord

"Hi [name] — I'd like to fit a retrofit smart lock on the inside of the front door. It clamps onto the existing thumb-turn with no drilling and no new holes, the outside of the door stays exactly as it is, and your key still works as normal. I'll remove it and restore the original thumb-turn when I move out."

If they ask for the model name, give it — and offer to show them the lock before you fit it. Most landlords say yes on the spot; the ones who say no are usually picturing a replacement lock, and the words "retrofit" and "interior only" are what change the answer.

What to check before you buy

Recreated illustration — five checks before you buy: deadbolt type, thumb-turn shape, door thickness, battery life, and the dead-battery plan.

  1. Single-cylinder deadbolt. All five picks fit most standard US single-cylinder deadbolts — key on the outside, thumb-turn on the inside. If your door has a double-cylinder deadbolt (key on both sides), a mortise lock, or no deadbolt at all, a retrofit lock will not fit.
  2. Thumb-turn shape. Most retrofit kits include adapters for round, lever and oval thumb-turns, but check the listing for your shape before you order — an adapter you do not have is a lock you cannot fit.
  3. Door thickness. Check the listing's supported range against your door. Most US apartment doors are standard, but older buildings vary.
  4. Battery life — and the dead-battery plan. Yale Approach lasts 9–12 months; SwitchBot Lock Ultra around 9; Aqara U200 6–8; SwitchBot Lock Pro 6–9; August 3–6. Every pick warns you weeks before the battery runs out — and when it does die, your physical key still opens the door from the outside and the thumb-turn still works from the inside. A dead battery is an inconvenience, never a lockout.
  5. Subscription. None of the five picks needs a monthly subscription for basic locking, guest codes or auto-lock. If a listing tries to sell you one, you do not need it.
  6. Wi-Fi vs Bluetooth. August and Yale Approach have Wi-Fi built in, so remote access works out of the box. Aqara U200 needs a Thread border router for remote access. SwitchBot locks work over Bluetooth nearby and need a hub for remote access and Matter.

Frequently asked questions

Do retrofit locks work with any deadbolt?

They fit most standard US single-cylinder deadbolts — key on the outside, thumb-turn on the inside. They do not fit double-cylinder deadbolts (key on both sides), mortise locks, or doors with no deadbolt. Check the thumb-turn shape and door thickness on the listing before you buy; most kits include adapters for the common shapes.

Does my landlord's key still work?

Yes. A retrofit lock clamps onto the thumb-turn on the inside of the door and never touches the cylinder, so the original key opens the door exactly as it always did — including in an emergency, when the battery is dead, or after you have moved out and removed the lock.

Can I install it without drilling?

Yes. Retrofit locks use the deadbolt's existing mounting screws — a screwdriver, about ten minutes, no new holes. The SwitchBot Lock Pro goes further and mounts with 3M adhesive: no tools at all, and it peels off cleanly at move-out.

What happens when the battery dies?

Nothing dramatic. Your physical key still opens the door from the outside and the thumb-turn still works from the inside — a dead battery is an inconvenience, never a lockout. Every pick warns you weeks ahead (low-battery alerts in the app), and the SwitchBot Lock Ultra carries an emergency cell as a backup.

Do I need a hub?

It depends on the lock. August Wi-Fi Smart Lock (4th Gen) and Yale Approach have Wi-Fi built in — no hub. Aqara U200 needs a Thread border router (Apple TV 4K or HomePod mini) for remote access. SwitchBot locks work over Bluetooth nearby and need a SwitchBot hub for Matter and remote access.

Can I take it when I move?

Yes — that is the point. Unclamp the lock, refit the original thumb-turn (keep it in a labelled bag while the smart lock is fitted), and the door is back to the way you found it in about ten minutes. The lock moves to your next flat; the deposit stays intact.
