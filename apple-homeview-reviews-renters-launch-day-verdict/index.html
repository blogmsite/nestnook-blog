The short answer

Apple HomeView goes on sale today, Friday 13 November 2026, for $299. The first reviews are good — Engadget gives it 8.4 out of 10, TechRadar 4 out of 5 — and every reviewer agrees on two things: the screen and the speaker are excellent, and the software is not finished yet.

For renters, three findings matter more than the scores:

  1. The privacy catch is real. When your iPhone is at home on the same Wi-Fi, HomeView pulls your personal text messages onto a shared kitchen screen. The Verge's reviewer could read her 15-year-old daughter's texts — and her daughter could read hers. In a flat with roommates, that is the deciding question.
  2. The wall model is not $299. It needs a mount and a power solution bought separately, and the total "costs nearly $400 with required accessories". Some mounts are not even available to order yet. Buy the tabletop model, or do not buy one at all.
  3. Siri cannot control your lock yet. Apple says a software update will fix a Siri AI issue that stops Siri from controlling smart-home products like locks — but the update is not coming until next month. If hands-free lock control is the reason you want one, wait.

Bottom line for renters: the tabletop HomeView is the first Apple smart-home display that is genuinely renter-safe — nothing goes into a wall, and it moves with you. It is also a first-generation product with a beta voice assistant, a shared-screen privacy problem, and a lock-control bug. Buy it today for the screen, the speaker and the hub. Do not buy it today for the Siri promises.

The pattern is consistent: hardware, 9 out of 10. Software, unfinished. That is the normal shape of a first-generation Apple product — and it is exactly why the renter's decision is "buy the tabletop model for what it does today" rather than "buy the promise".

What the reviewers agree HomeView does well:

  • Smart-home control. The Home view shows your lights, locks, thermostats and cameras on one glanceable screen, and it can show up to nine live security-camera feeds at once.
  • The kitchen. "An excellent hands-free kitchen companion" — timers, recipes, music and FaceTime while your hands are full.
  • The speaker. Between a HomePod mini and a full-size HomePod, with a modest Dolby Atmos effect. The tabletop model has two full-range drivers, a down-firing woofer and dual passive radiators; the wall model has two full-range drivers only.
  • The photo frame. A dedicated Photos face pulls from your iCloud Photos library or Shared Albums. Gear Patrol calls it Apple's first digital picture frame.
  • Setup. Bring your iPhone near, point its camera at the screen, do a face scan, say a few phrases for Siri — done in minutes.

What the reviewers agree is unfinished:

  • Siri AI is a beta, English-only at launch, and still slow at times. Tech journalist Joanna Stern noted in her newsletter that Siri refused a smart-home command during her review.
  • No App Store. Only pre-installed Apple apps (Messages, Weather, Alarms, Calendar, FaceTime, Safari) plus CarPlay-enabled audio and video apps such as Spotify, YouTube and Netflix. Apple TV is the only video app, and it plays in a narrow band with black bars.
  • No physical buttons. "When I want to adjust the volume quickly or mute Siri, I have to tap the display or pull out my phone" (The Verge).
  • It needs your iPhone nearby. Personal content — messages, calendar, reminders — depends on a nearby, recently unlocked iPhone or iPad. Without one, HomeView is a much dumber screen.

The privacy catch: your texts on a shared screen

This is the finding that matters most to renters, and it is the one no launch coverage has framed for shared flats yet.

HomeView is built to sit in a shared room — the kitchen counter, a hallway table, a bedside — and be used by everyone in the household. It recognises up to six people by face and shows each of them their own messages, calendar and reminders. That is the design. Here is the catch, from The Verge's launch review:

When a user's iPhone or iPad is at home on the same Wi-Fi network, the display acts as an extension of that device, pulling in personal text messages, calendars, and reminders. Putting personal messages onto a shared kitchen screen created immediate friction during testing. "Clunky authentication checks and being sent to my iPhone too many times interrupt the hands-free experience. Not to mention that I could easily read my 15-year-old daughter's text messages, and vice versa."

Apple's privacy design is that HomeView shows sensitive personal information only after recognising your face and detecting a nearby, recently unlocked iPhone or iPad paired to you, with facial and voice recognition processed inside protected hardware on the A18 chip. In practice, the reviewer found the authentication checks clunky — and the failure mode is that one person's messages become visible to the household.

What this means for renters:

  • Flat with roommates: this is the biggest risk. A shared kitchen screen that can surface one flatmate's messages is a problem no other smart display creates in quite this way, because no other display is this deeply tied to your phone. If you share a kitchen with people you are not family with, think hard before putting HomeView there — or put it in your bedroom instead, where the audience is you.
  • Couples and families: the face-recognition system is designed for this, and the six-person limit covers most households. But the reviewer's experience — a parent reading a teenager's texts — shows the boundary is porous in daily use.
  • Solo renters: the catch barely applies. If you live alone, the shared-screen problem does not exist, and HomeView's privacy design is a strength: face recognition on-device, no cloud processing of your face or voice.

The renter's rule: HomeView belongs in a room whose audience you control. In a shared flat, that is your bedroom, not the kitchen. If the kitchen is where you wanted it, the Echo Show 5 — which does not pull your texts onto its screen — is the safer shared-room choice.

The real cost: the wall model is nearly $400, not $299

Both HomeView models list at $299. Only one of them costs $299.

The tabletop model sits on a counter, plugs into a normal outlet, and needs nothing else. The wall-mounted model needs a mount and a power solution bought separately — and the total "costs nearly $400 with required accessories", according to the launch-day roundup. The mounts start at $59.95, and the picture is worse than the price:

  • The PoE mount (power over Ethernet) is not available until early 2027.
  • The AC Wired mount shows as unavailable to order.
  • The wall model has weaker speakers — two full-range drivers only, against the tabletop's two drivers plus a down-firing woofer and dual passive radiators.

For a renter, the wall model fails twice: it costs more, sounds worse, and it asks you to mount something on a wall you do not own — with hardware you cannot even buy yet. The tabletop model is the only one a renter can actually use on day one, and it is the only one this guide recommends.

The Siri lock bug: if you want hands-free locks, wait

This is the second reason to pause before buying today.

Apple says a software update will fix a Siri AI issue that stops Siri from controlling smart-home products like locks — but that update is not coming until next month. If hands-free lock control is the reason you want a HomeView, the device cannot do that job yet.

The software picture in full:

  • HomeView ships with older 26.5 software and prompts you to update to version 27.2 during setup.
  • Apple released a pre-launch update on 12 November fixing nine security vulnerabilities — update before you use it.
  • Siri AI is a beta, available in English initially. Personal-context requests — acting on your messages, calendar and apps — need a paired Apple Intelligence-capable iPhone or iPad with Siri AI switched on.
  • Apple says certain server-based Siri features have daily usage limits, with expanded access planned for a future fee that has not been announced yet. The $299 price does not necessarily buy you unlimited Siri AI.
  • HomeView is not sold in the European Union — Siri AI is currently unavailable in iOS there.

None of this makes HomeView a bad product. It makes it a first-generation product with a beta assistant. The screen, the speaker and the hub work today. The voice assistant that justifies the premium over an Echo Show 5 does not fully work yet.

If you buy today, buy it for the screen, the speaker and the hub — and treat the Siri improvements as a free upgrade when they arrive. If you are buying it to control your lock by voice, wait for the December update and the first reports that it actually works.

What it does well for renters — and what it does not

The case for buying it today:

  • It is genuinely renter-safe. The tabletop model sits on a counter and plugs into a normal outlet. Nothing is screwed into a wall, nothing is wired into your electrics, and it moves with you when your lease ends. Pack it in its box and it works in the next flat on day one.
  • It is a real hub. Wi-Fi 6E, Bluetooth 6 and Thread are built in, and it works as an Apple home hub (Matter/Thread). One $299 device replaces a speaker, a display and a hub — and if you already own a HomePod mini 2 or an Apple TV 4K, your Thread network is already there.
  • Nine camera feeds at once. The Home Camera face shows up to nine live security-camera feeds on one screen. For a renter with a doorbell camera, a hallway camera and a balcony camera, that is the whole flat on one glanceable display.
  • It is a kitchen device first. Hands-free timers, music, FaceTime and smart-home control while you cook — the use case every reviewer praised most.
  • Spotify works. HomeView has no App Store, but it mirrors CarPlay-enabled apps from a nearby paired iPhone, so Spotify, YouTube and Netflix all work. Built-in apps are Apple's own only.

The case for waiting:

  • Siri AI is a beta and cannot control your lock until December. If voice control is the point, wait.
  • The privacy catch matters in any shared flat. A screen that can show one person's messages to the household needs a room you control.
  • The price is not the whole story. Wall mounting costs nearly $400 in practice, some Siri features need a newer iPhone or iPad paired to the display, and Apple's own footnotes say expanded access to some AI features will eventually cost extra.
  • It is not sold in the EU, and it is a first-generation product. The second generation will fix the rough edges — the question is whether you want to pay $299 to find them first.

Apple Home Collection: orderable today, arriving in December

Apple also confirmed that select Apple Home Collection accessories will be available to order starting today, Friday 13 November, with the first products arriving in December. The line — designed by LG and Schneider Electric in collaboration with Apple — includes indoor and outdoor cameras, thermostats, temperature sensors, a video doorbell and a door lock. All of them work with HomeView, and all of them are Matter.

Two details matter for renters:

  • The Indoor Cam has a privacy-preserving feature that lets it tilt up when people are in the home so it is not recording, and down when they leave — a direct answer to the shared-screen privacy problem above.
  • Some accessories will not be released until early 2027, so not everything is available to order today.

The renter question nobody has answered yet: which of these are no-drill, and which need a landlord's permission? A camera that sticks to a shelf is renter-safe; a door lock that replaces your deadbolt is not, and neither is a thermostat that needs wiring. NestNook will cover each accessory as it ships — the December arrival dates are the next thing to watch.

If you buy one: the renter's launch-day checklist

  1. Buy the tabletop model. Silver, starlight, rose pink or space gray — the colour does not matter, the base does. Do not buy the wall model.
  2. Update before you use it. HomeView ships on older 26.5 software and prompts an update to 27.2 during setup. Apple released a pre-launch update on 12 November fixing nine security vulnerabilities — install it first.
  3. Put it in a room you control. In a shared flat, that is your bedroom, not the shared kitchen — until you are comfortable with how the shared-text handling behaves in daily use.
  4. Check your iPhone. Setup needs an iPhone on iOS 27.2+ or an iPad on iPadOS 27.2+, and personal Siri features need an Apple Intelligence-capable iPhone or iPad with Siri AI switched on. If your phone is older, HomeView is a much dumber screen.
  5. Do not buy it for the lock — yet. Siri cannot control smart-home locks until the December update. Test it after the update lands before you rely on it.
  6. Keep the box. You will move. The tabletop HomeView packs flat, plugs into a normal outlet, and works in the next flat on day one — that is the whole renter argument for it.
  7. Budget for the future fee. Apple says expanded access to some Siri AI features will eventually cost extra, and the fee has not been announced. The $299 is the entry price, not necessarily the total cost.

Frequently asked questions

Is Apple HomeView good for renters?

Yes — the tabletop model. It sits on a counter, plugs into a normal outlet, needs nothing screwed into a wall, and moves with you when you move. The wall-mounted model is not renter-safe: it costs nearly $400 with the required mounts, some of which are not available to order yet, and it has weaker speakers.

Can my roommates see my texts on HomeView?

Possibly — and that is the biggest catch in the launch reviews. When your iPhone is at home on the same Wi-Fi, HomeView pulls your personal text messages, calendars and reminders onto its screen. The Verge's reviewer could read her daughter's texts, and vice versa. In a shared flat, put HomeView in your bedroom, not the shared kitchen.

Does Spotify work on Apple HomeView?

Yes. HomeView has no App Store, but it mirrors CarPlay-enabled apps from a nearby paired iPhone — so Spotify, YouTube and Netflix all work. Built-in apps are Apple's own only, and Apple TV is the only video app, playing in a narrow band with black bars.

Can Siri on HomeView control my smart lock?

Not yet. Apple says a software update will fix a Siri AI issue that stops Siri from controlling smart-home products like locks, but the update is not coming until December 2026. If hands-free lock control is your reason to buy, wait.

How much does the wall-mounted HomeView really cost?

Nearly $400. Both models list at $299, but the wall model needs a mount and a power solution bought separately (from $59.95), and the PoE mount is not available until early 2027. The wall model also has weaker speakers than the tabletop model.

Is HomeView better than the Echo Show 5 for a rented flat?

It depends on your phone. If you live in Apple's world and want a 7-inch display, a good speaker and a Thread hub, HomeView is the better screen. If you want the best value, or you share a kitchen with roommates, the Echo Show 5 ($89.99, was $49.99 on Prime Day) is cheaper, simpler, and does not put your texts on a shared screen.

What is the Apple Home Collection, and when can I order it?

A new line of Matter accessories — indoor and outdoor cameras, thermostats, temperature sensors, a video doorbell and a door lock — designed by LG and Schneider Electric with Apple. Select accessories are orderable from today, 13 November 2026, with availability beginning in December; some arrive in early 2027.
